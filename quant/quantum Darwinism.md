Zurek's answer to why the classical world looks objective: the environment does not merely destroy [[quantum]] superposition, it *copies* the surviving pointer states many times over. a fact is objective exactly when many independent fragments of the environment each carry a full record of it

the measurable signature is a redundancy plateau — the mutual information between the system and a fragment saturates once the fragment is large enough, so many observers can learn the same fact independently without disturbing it. seen in photonic and NV-center experiments (2019)

this is the physics of [[cyberank]]. focus on a [[particle]] is high precisely when many independent [[neurons]] have imprinted it, so $\phi^*$ measures redundancy of records — objectivity in Zurek's sense, priced

it also supplies a test [[cyber]] does not yet run: measure how many independent neuron-fragments are needed before a particle's rank stops changing. a fast plateau means the fact is objective; a slow one means the consensus is thin and one actor still owns it
